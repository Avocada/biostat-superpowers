02 / Calculation

The association is positive; causation is unresolved.

Day-1 RHC was associated with higher recorded 30-day mortality: 830/2,184 (38.0%) versus 1,088/3,551 (30.6%). The crude risk difference was 7.36 (95% CI 4.83 to 9.90) percentage points; the risk ratio was 1.24 (95% CI 1.15 to 1.33).

Exploratory legacy IPTW. Weighted mortality proportions: 36.6% with RHC and 31.6% without RHC. RD 5.00 (95% CI 2.20 to 8.40) percentage points; RR 1.16 (95% CI 1.07 to 1.28). All 5,735 patients remain. The stabilized weights are normalized separately within each group when calculating risks. These are weighted associations; the intended ATE interpretation is not identified.

The all-covariate logistic regression gave a conditional adjusted OR of 1.37 (model-based 95% CI 1.18–1.59; convergence: True). This is neither an RR nor g-computation and is not the primary result. Its model-based interval also conditions on the single-imputation design.

04 / Legacy audit

What the script calculates—and what its labels overstate.

Legacy claim / choice Audit finding
“All baseline covariates” Excluding named outcomes and dates does not establish covariate timing. The reproduction uses the legacy remaining fields only to audit its arithmetic.
“Trim to overlap” np.clip changes probabilities to [0.02, 0.98]. It excludes zero patients. This modifies weights and can introduce bias; it does not restore identification or define a restricted overlap population.
“G-computation-style OR” The treatment coefficient from the logistic model is a conditional odds ratio, not a standardized marginal risk contrast or g-computation. Odds ratios are noncollapsible and mortality is common here.
“Mean balance improved” A mean absolute SMD can hide individual failures. We save every term, show the largest initial imbalances and report maximum residual SMD. We fix the denominator at the unweighted pooled population SD; the legacy weighted-SD denominator changes the yardstick.
Single imputation + no intervals The primary crude comparison requires no imputation. The exploratory weighted interval refits imputation and propensity estimation in a patient bootstrap, but still does not account for a scientifically justified missing-data model or systematic bias.
Network-dependent input The original file is preserved unchanged. The estimator was transcribed to read identical supplied bytes offline; this is not an unchanged-script execution and not a reproduction of the original published matching analysis.
05 / Interpretation gates

Why this is not a completed causal analysis.

Timing · critical

The dictionary defines swang1 on the first day a patient qualified for SUPPORT; the publication describes the first 24 hours of ICU care. These windows are not proven interchangeable. The snapshot has no within-day treatment or covariate timestamps. Day-1 physiology, DNR status and prognostic scores cannot all be certified as pretreatment. An exposure assigned over a day can introduce immortal-time or reverse-causation bias if follow-up starts earlier. Obtain the original timing definitions and records, then specify a grace-period strategy; do not assume a landmark analysis would estimate the same target.

Identification · critical

Severity and clinical indications influence both catheterization and death. Unrecorded clinician judgment, treatment decisions and hospital practice may confound the comparison. In a conceptual DAG, pretreatment severity → RHC and mortality; RHC → later physiology → mortality. Adjusting for later physiology could block mediation or open collider paths. No sufficient adjustment set has been established. Exchangeability, consistency, treatment-version definitions, positivity and no interference remain assumptions, not findings.

Missing data · high

cat2 is blank in 4,535/5,735 (79.1%), adld3p in 4,296 (74.9%), and urin1 in 3,028 (52.8%). A blank secondary diagnosis may mean no secondary diagnosis, but the dictionary does not confirm this. Legacy dummy encoding pools blanks with the reference category; numeric median imputation suppresses missing-value uncertainty and can distort associations. Missing indicators and omission sensitivities do not establish MAR or repair MNAR. Resolve structural blanks and timing before developing outcome-inclusive multiple imputation and clinically justified MNAR sensitivity.

Model and transport · high

The legacy propensity model is an additive, L2-regularized logistic model with default C=1; no nonlinearities or interactions are assessed. Good balance on included columns does not guarantee balance on joint distributions or unmeasured factors. This historical cohort came from five US teaching hospitals, 1989–1994; it does not directly represent current ICU practice. The snapshot lacks site IDs, so site clustering is not addressed.

Review · unresolved

This is an analyst self-audit. No human scientific approval or independent-context review has occurred. The requested later independent review remains pending. Controller reporting readiness is not asserted; this document records calculations and unresolved gates.

Proposed next work: establish the exact study-day origin and within-day measurement order; adjudicate structural blanks and sentinel values; define the treatment strategy and causal DAG; obtain a clinically justified adjustment set and missing-data plan; then reassess balance, positivity, uncertainty, and independent review. No bootstrap or model change can substitute for these steps.
