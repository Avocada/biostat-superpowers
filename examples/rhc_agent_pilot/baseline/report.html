SUPPORT / RHC · Observational study audit

Higher mortality.
Unresolved causality.

5,735 patients · 30-day outcome · local analysis

Day-1 RHC was associated with higher recorded 30-day mortality: 38.0% versus 30.6%, an observed difference of 7.36 percentage points (95% CI 4.83 to 9.90). This comparison does not establish that RHC caused the difference.

01 / Population and question

A cohort comparison, with a defined denominator

The primary estimand is the difference and ratio of recorded 30-day death proportions between patients coded RHC and No RHC in the supplied 5,735-person cohort. Unit: one patient; exposure: swang1; outcome: dth30 = Yes. All supplied patients are retained. No RHC means no day-1 RHC, not necessarily never receiving RHC. No predictive train/test split is needed for this descriptive analysis.

Checks: 0 duplicate rows; unique patient IDs; 0 disagreements between dth30 and recorded death-date minus admission-date ≤30 days; 0 negative death intervals; 0 non-deaths with t3d30 below 30; 11 non-deaths with last contact before day 30. Minimum t3d30 is 2 days. These are consistency checks, not source-record verification. Date units are treated as the numeric day counts supplied.

04 / Critical review

What the legacy script gets wrong

Legacy choiceCorrection / interpretation
Excludes only IDs, dates and outcomes; calls everything else baselineA column exclusion list cannot establish pre-treatment availability or distinguish confounders, mediators and colliders. The estimator is reproduced solely for audit.
Calls 0.02/0.98 clipping “trim to overlap”All patients remain. Scores are winsorized; weights and resulting target comparison are altered.
Calls a logistic treatment coefficient “g-computation-style”That coefficient is a conditional odds ratio, not a marginal standardized risk contrast. With common mortality, OR is not RR; noncollapsibility also prevents simple comparison. That regression is not rerun here.
Reports only mean SMD; changes SD denominator after weightingMean imbalance can hide individual failures. This audit uses a fixed SD and saves every encoded term, plus maximum SMD, tails and effective sample size.
Median imputation with no uncertainty intervalsThe reproduction now has a full-pipeline bootstrap, but single imputation remains scientifically unresolved; the interval is not an MI-pooled interval.
Describes causal validity as exchangeability plus positivityConsistency, well-defined interventions, time alignment, appropriate measurement/selection, missing-data assumptions and model adequacy also matter. An E-value is not a readiness certificate.

05 / Identification and next decisions

Why a causal interpretation is not ready

Causal readiness: not_ready. Descriptive calculation is complete. The legacy causal claim is not scientifically approved. This is a qualified analysis audit, not a finalized causal report or a clinical recommendation.

A hypothetical target trial would enroll eligible critically ill patients at the first SUPPORT-qualifying time, assign a specified strategy of RHC during day 1 versus no RHC during day 1, and compare 30-day mortality from that same time. The desired contrast could be the marginal ATE on risk-difference and risk-ratio scales in eligible patients. A day-1 grace period, subsequent treatment/crossover, intervention versions and early deaths need explicit rules; the supplied extract cannot resolve them.

Working causal structure (hypothesis, not verified): pre-treatment severity and chronic illness → RHC and mortality; unmeasured clinical judgment → RHC and mortality; RHC → later physiology → mortality. Conditioning on physiology after RHC could block mediation or open collider paths. No sufficient adjustment set is certified here, and no biological direction or bias magnitude is inferred from this sketch.
